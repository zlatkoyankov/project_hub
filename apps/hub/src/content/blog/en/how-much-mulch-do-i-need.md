Mulch keeps soil moist, holds back weeds and makes beds look finished. Buying the right amount is mostly a question of area and depth. Too thin and weeds push through; too thick and it can smother plants. Here is how to work it out, with examples you can check in the [mulch calculator](/calculators/mulch-calculator/).

## How deep should mulch be?

For most garden beds, a layer of **2–3 inches (5–8 cm)** is the usual recommendation. Finer mulches can be a little thinner, coarse bark a little thicker. Around trees and shrubs, keep the mulch a few inches away from the trunk or stems; piling it against bark keeps it wet and invites rot and pests.

If you are topping up last year's mulch, measure what is left and add only the difference.

## The formula

**volume = area × depth**

With depth in feet (3 inches = 0.25 ft) the result is in cubic feet. Bagged mulch is sold in cubic feet; bulk mulch in cubic yards (27 cubic feet each).

## Example 1: a rectangular bed, 20 × 4 ft, 3 inches deep

1. Area: 20 × 4 = 80 ft²
2. Volume: 80 × 0.25 = **20 cubic feet**
3. In cubic yards: 20 ÷ 27 = 0.74 yd³

Mulch bags are commonly 2 cubic feet (also 1.5 and 3, so check the label): 20 ÷ 2 = **10 bags**.

## Example 2: a curved border

Curved beds look awkward to measure, but you only need a good approximation:

1. Measure the length along the middle of the border: say 30 ft.
2. Measure the width at three or four points and take the average: say 3.5 ft.
3. Area ≈ 30 × 3.5 = 105 ft²
4. At 3 inches: 105 × 0.25 = 26.3 cubic feet, so **14 bags** of 2 cubic feet.

For very irregular shapes, split the bed into rectangles and triangles, calculate each, and add them up. The same method is explained in [how to measure square footage](/blog/how-to-measure-square-footage/).

## Example 3: a ring around a tree

A ring is a circle, so the area is π × radius². For a ring 6 ft across (radius 3 ft):

1. Area: 3.1416 × 3 × 3 = 28.3 ft²
2. At 3 inches: 28.3 × 0.25 = **7.1 cubic feet**, about 4 bags

Strictly, you could subtract the trunk, but leaving the gap around it unmulched more than covers that.

## Metric example: 6 × 1.2 m bed, 7 cm deep

1. Area: 6 × 1.2 = 7.2 m²
2. Volume: 7.2 × 0.07 = 0.50 m³, or **504 litres**
3. In 60-litre bags: 504 ÷ 60 = 8.4, so **9 bags**

## Bags or bulk?

Bags are easy to carry and store, and you can buy exactly what you need. Bulk mulch, delivered by the cubic yard or cubic metre, is usually cheaper once you need more than a couple of cubic yards. As a rough guide, one cubic yard equals about 13–14 bags of 2 cubic feet. Ask your supplier for both prices, including delivery.

## A few practical tips

- **Weed and water first.** Mulch keeps moisture in, so lay it on damp soil after weeding.
- **Edge the beds.** A clean edge stops mulch from spilling onto lawns and paths.
- **Keep some spare.** A few extra bags help to fill thin spots after the first heavy rain.
