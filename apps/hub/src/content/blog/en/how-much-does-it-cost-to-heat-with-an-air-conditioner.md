An air conditioner that can heat, which in the US is usually called a heat pump or mini-split, typically uses far less electricity than a plug-in heater for the same warmth. In our example below, a 12,000 Btu unit costs about $1.51 a day, or $45 a month at the 2026 US average price, against $2.26 a day for a 1,500 W space heater, which gives only half the heat in the heat pump's best case.

## Why it uses less electricity

A space heater or an electric radiator turns electricity straight into heat: 1 kWh in, about 1 kWh of heat out.

A heat pump moves heat from the outdoor air into the room instead of making it. According to the US Department of Energy, an air-source heat pump can deliver up to three times more heat energy than the electrical energy it consumes. That is the best case. The ratio falls as it gets colder outside, and it depends on the model. In the US it shows up on the label as HSPF (and on European labels as SCOP): a higher number means cheaper heating.

## The formula

**kWh = power (W) ÷ 1,000 × hours**

**Cost = kWh × price per kWh**

For a heat pump, use the electrical power it draws, not the heat it delivers. Both are on the datasheet. Also, an inverter unit does not run flat out all the time: once the room is warm it slows down, so the average draw is lower than the maximum.

## Worked example

The calculator's air conditioner preset is 1,000 W for 8 hours. It is based on a 12,000 Btu inverter unit whose datasheet lists 0.94 kW of electrical input when heating (a Bulgarian retailer's listing, in English). The average US price was 18.83 cents per kWh in April 2026 (EIA).

| | Energy | Cost at 18.83¢ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heat pump, 1,000 W, 8 h, per day | 8 kWh | $1.51 |
| Heat pump, 30 days | 240 kWh | $45.19 |
| Same, but running at 60% on average | 144 kWh | $27.12 a month |
| Space heater, 1,500 W, 8 h, per day | 12 kWh | $2.26 |
| Space heater, 30 days | 360 kWh | $67.79 |

Now compare the heat. In the best case of three units of heat per unit of electricity, 8 kWh of electricity gives 24 kWh of heat. Making the same 24 kWh with a resistance heater costs 24 × $0.1883 = **$4.52 a day**, three times as much. In a colder spell, when the ratio drops to 2, the heat pump delivers 16 kWh from the same 8 kWh and the heater would cost $3.01 for that heat: still half the electricity. The ratios 2 and 3 are illustrations, not measurements of any particular unit.

## Ways to spend less

- **Set a moderate temperature** rather than a high one. Every extra degree makes the unit work harder.
- **Avoid constant on-off cycling.** Inverter units are most efficient holding a steady temperature rather than reheating a cold room from scratch.
- **Clean the filters.** A clogged filter cuts airflow and efficiency.
- **Close doors** to rooms you are not heating and seal draughts around windows.
- **Keep the outdoor unit clear** of snow, ice and leaves.
