Self-leveling compound, floor screed and plaster all come as dry mix in bags, and they are all estimated the same way: area × thickness × how much mix the product uses per millimeter. The hard part is not the arithmetic. It is getting the thickness right.

## The formula

Manufacturers give consumption as kilograms of dry mix per square meter for each millimeter of thickness. Typical figures from data sheets:

| Material | Dry mix per m² per mm | Per sq ft per 1/8 in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cement screed | about 2.0 kg | about 1.3 lb |
| Self-leveling compound | about 1.8 kg | about 1.2 lb |
| Gypsum plaster | about 0.8 to 1.0 kg | about 0.6 lb |

**dry mix (kg) = area (m²) × thickness (mm) × consumption**

Then add a waste allowance, about 10%, and divide by the bag weight.

## Measure the average thickness

Floors and walls are rarely flat, and the thickness changes across the room. Lay a long straightedge or use a laser level, measure the gap in several places and take the average. On a floor that is 1/8 in low at one end and 1/2 in low at the other, the average is about 5/16 in, not 1/8 in. Using the thinnest point is the most common reason for running out halfway through a pour.

## Worked example: leveling a floor

A 150 sq ft room (about 13.9 m²) needs an average of 1/4 in (6.35 mm) of self-leveling compound:

1. 13.9 × 6.35 × 1.8 = 159 kg of dry mix.
2. With 10% waste: 175 kg, about 386 lb.
3. In 50 lb bags: 386 ÷ 50 = 7.7, so **8 bags**.

## Worked example: plastering walls

37 m² of walls (about 400 sq ft) with 12 mm (1/2 in) of gypsum plaster:

1. 37 × 12 × 0.9 = 400 kg.
2. With 10% waste: 440 kg.
3. In 30 kg bags: **15 bags**.

## Tips

- **Use your product's figure.** The bag or the data sheet gives the exact consumption; enter it in the calculator for an exact count.
- **Have every bag ready for a self-leveling pour.** It is poured in one go and starts to set within minutes, so you cannot stop to buy more.
- **Prime the floor** with the primer the manufacturer specifies, or the compound can lose water into the subfloor and crack.
- **Check the thickness limits.** Each product has a minimum and maximum thickness per layer. Thicker fills may need a different product or several layers.
