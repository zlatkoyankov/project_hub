Almost every home project starts with the same number: how many square feet (or square metres) is the room? Flooring, tile, underlay, paint for the ceiling, even the size of a heater depend on it. For a simple rectangle it takes a minute. For an L-shaped living room with a bay window it takes five, if you know the trick. Here it is, with examples you can check in the [square footage calculator](/calculators/square-footage-calculator/).

## What you need

- A tape measure, or a laser measure for larger rooms
- Paper and a pencil to sketch the room
- A calculator, or the one linked above

Measure wall to wall at floor level. Skirting boards are fine to measure over; for flooring, the few millimetres they hide are covered by your waste allowance anyway.

## Rectangles: length × width

A room 14 ft long and 11 ft wide:

14 × 11 = **154 square feet**

In metres: 4.2 × 3.4 = **14.28 m²**

If the walls are not quite parallel (common in older houses), measure each dimension in two places and use the larger value.

## L-shaped rooms: split them up

The trick for any odd shape is to divide it into rectangles, calculate each, and add them up.

Say a living room is 20 ft along one wall and 12 ft along the other, but one corner has an 8 × 6 ft piece missing where the hallway is.

**Method A: add the parts**

- Part 1: 20 × 6 = 120 ft²
- Part 2: 12 × 6 = 72 ft²
- Total: **192 ft²**

**Method B: subtract the missing piece**

- Full rectangle: 20 × 12 = 240 ft²
- Missing corner: 8 × 6 = 48 ft²
- Total: 240 − 48 = **192 ft²**

Both give the same answer. Use whichever needs fewer measurements, and use the other one to check your work.

## Triangles and angled walls

A wall at an angle creates a triangle. Its area is half the base times the height:

**area = ½ × base × height**

A triangular corner with sides of 6 ft and 4 ft along the two straight walls: ½ × 6 × 4 = **12 ft²**.

## Circles and bay windows

A round area is π × radius². A semicircular bay with a radius of 3 ft is half a circle:

½ × 3.1416 × 3 × 3 = **14.1 ft²**

A three-sided bay window is easier to treat as a rectangle plus two small triangles.

## Converting between square feet and square metres

- 1 m² = 10.764 ft²
- 1 ft² = 0.0929 m²

So 192 ft² is 192 × 0.0929 = **17.8 m²**, and a 20 m² room is 20 × 10.764 = **215 ft²**.

A common mistake is converting the length and width but forgetting to square the factor: 1 m is 3.28 ft, but 1 m² is 3.28 × 3.28 = 10.76 ft², not 3.28.

## What to include and what to leave out

It depends on the job:

- **Flooring:** include everything the floor will cover, including under removable furniture. Leave out fitted kitchen units and built-in wardrobes if the floor stops at their base. Then add a waste allowance; see [how much extra tile to buy](/blog/tile-waste-percentage/) for typical percentages.
- **Ceiling paint:** the floor area is the ceiling area, unless the ceiling slopes.
- **Heating:** use the full floor area and the ceiling height, because heaters are sized by volume and insulation.

## A quick routine

1. Sketch the room from above.
2. Divide it into rectangles, triangles and half-circles.
3. Write each measurement on the sketch.
4. Calculate each piece, then add or subtract.
5. Measure once more anything that seems odd.
