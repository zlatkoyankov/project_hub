Every crossword solver knows the feeling: half the grid is done and the rest stares back. Getting faster is less about knowing more words and more about working the grid in the right order. These habits help whether you solve a daily newspaper puzzle or a puzzle book on holiday.

## 1. Fill in the easy clues first

Do a quick pass and answer only what you are sure of: fill-in-the-blank clues, short words, abbreviations and anything you know at a glance. Do not stop on a hard clue in the first round. Every certain answer gives you letters in the words that cross it.

## 2. Read what kind of clue it is

The clue tells you more than its meaning:

- **Tense and number match.** A clue in the past tense has an answer in the past tense; a plural clue usually has a plural answer, often ending in S.
- **Abbreviations point to abbreviations.** "Org.", "abbr." or a shortened word in the clue usually means a shortened answer.
- **A question mark means wordplay.** The answer is a pun or an unusual reading of the clue, so do not take it literally.
- **Quotation marks** often mean a phrase someone would say, such as "I agree!" for YES.

## 3. Let the crossing words do the work

When you are stuck on a word, leave it and solve the words that cross it. Two or three letters in the right places often make the answer obvious. Experienced solvers move around the grid much more than beginners do.

## 4. Think in letter patterns

Write down what you have, with gaps for the missing squares: **C?O?S**. Then think of words that fit the shape rather than the clue alone. Common endings help here: -ING, -ED, -ER, -EST, -LY and -TION fill a lot of squares.

## 5. Try the common vowels and letters

If a gap is between two consonants, it is very likely a vowel. E, A, I, O and U, and among consonants R, S, T, L and N, appear far more often than the rest. Pencilling in a likely vowel can unlock the whole word.

## 6. Learn the regulars

Crossword setters reuse short words with convenient letters again and again: ERA, ORE, ALOE, OREO, AREA, EPEE and many more. After a few weeks of regular solving you will recognise them, and they give you free letters in the corners of the grid.

## 7. Take a break

It sounds too simple, but it works. Put the puzzle down for ten minutes and come back. You will often see an answer at once that you could not find before, because you stop reading the clue the same way.

## 8. Look it up, then learn it

There is no shame in checking a word when you are truly stuck, especially when you are learning. The trick is to learn from it: read what the word means, so next time you know it.
