From 1 January to 31 December 2027 there are **364 days**, or **365** if you count both dates. That one-day difference is the most common mistake with dates. The rest is knowing whether you need calendar days or working days.

## Days between two dates

**days = end date − start date**

This counts the start day but not the end day, like "in 10 days". Monday to the next Monday is 7 days.

If you need both ends counted, for example days of leave or nights plus one for a hotel stay, **add 1**.

Example: 11 November to 25 December 2027 is **44 days**. Counting both days it is 45.

## Weekdays only

Count the Mondays to Fridays in the span. 1 January to 31 December 2027 has **260 weekdays** (the 31st not counted).

Public holidays come out on top of that. In 2027 US federal offices have [12 days off](/us-federal-holidays-2027/), which leaves 249 federal working days. England and Wales have [8 bank holidays](/uk-bank-holidays-2027/), which leaves 253.

## A date 30, 60 or 90 days later

Add the days to the start date and let the months roll over. Month lengths differ, so "90 days" and "3 months" are not the same:

- **90 days after 1 March 2027** is **30 May 2027**.
- **3 months after 1 March 2027** is 1 June 2027.

If a contract says days, count days. If it says months, the date moves to the same day of the month.

## February and leap years

February 2027 has 28 days, February 2028 has 29. A year is a leap year if it divides by 4, except century years, which must divide by 400. So 2000 was a leap year and 2100 will not be.

1 February to 1 March is 28 days in 2027 and 29 days in 2028.

## How deadlines are usually counted

For legal deadlines, the day of the event that starts the period is usually **not** counted. In US federal courts, for example, you exclude the day of the event, count every day including weekends and holidays, and include the last day; if the last day is a weekend or legal holiday, the period runs to the next working day. Other courts, contracts and countries can have their own rules, so check the one that applies to you.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting both ends without meaning to.** "From the 1st to the 10th" is 9 days apart, but 10 days if both are included.
- **Treating 3 months as 90 days.** It is 89 to 92 days, depending on the months.
- **Forgetting holidays in working days.** Weekdays are not the same as working days.
- **Forgetting that the end day is excluded in "within 14 days".** Read the rule for your deadline.
