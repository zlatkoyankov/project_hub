The obvious way to work out wallpaper is to divide the wall area by the area of one roll. It is also the most common reason people run out halfway around the room. Wallpaper is hung in full-height strips, and what really decides the number of rolls is how many whole strips you can cut from each one.

## Why the area method falls short

Take a 12 × 10 ft room with 8 ft walls and one door. The walls are 352 sq ft, minus about 21 sq ft for the door, so 331 sq ft. A US double roll of 20.5 in × 33 ft covers about 56 sq ft, so the area method says 331 ÷ 56 = **6 rolls**.

Now count strips. Each strip is the wall height plus about 4 in for trimming at the ceiling and the floor, so 8 ft 4 in. A 33 ft roll gives **3 strips**, with about 8 ft left over that is too short for another one. The room needs 25 strips, which is **9 rolls**, half as many again as the area method said.

## Step 1: count the strips

Measure around the room and divide by the roll width:

- Perimeter: 2 × (12 + 10) = 44 ft.
- Doors and windows save whole strips across their width. Roughly, their area divided by the wall height: 21 ÷ 8 ≈ 2.6 ft.
- 44 − 2.6 = 41.4 ft ÷ 20.5 in = 24.2, so **25 strips**.

## Step 2: strips per roll

Divide the roll length by the strip length and round **down**:

33 ft ÷ 8 ft 4 in = 3.96, so **3 strips** per roll.

## Step 3: rolls

25 ÷ 3 = 8.3, which rounds up to **9 rolls**. Buy one more from the same batch for repairs and mistakes.

## The pattern repeat

Patterned paper has a repeat: the distance after which the pattern starts again. Every strip has to start at the same point in the pattern, so strips are cut in whole repeats. With a 21 in repeat and 8 ft walls, each strip needs 5 repeats (105 in) plus trim instead of 96 in. With a large repeat you lose a strip per roll more often, so always use the repeat from the label.

Half-drop patterns, where the pattern lines up half a repeat lower on the next strip, waste even more. Plain papers and random-match designs waste the least.

## Before you start

- **Check the batch number** on every roll. Rolls from different batches can differ slightly in colour.
- **Hang a test strip** to check the pattern match and the colour in your room's light.
- **Paste:** one pack usually covers several rolls. The pack says how many.
