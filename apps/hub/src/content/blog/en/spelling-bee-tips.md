The Spelling Bee gives you seven letters in a honeycomb. Every word must use the centre letter, be at least four letters long and use only those seven letters, which can repeat. A word that uses all seven is a pangram. These habits help you go from a handful of words to Genius.

## 1. Start with the endings

Look for common endings you can build: **-ING, -ED, -ER, -LY, -TION, -ABLE**. If the letters allow -ING, try putting almost every other combination in front of it. One good ending can unlock a dozen words.

## 2. Try prefixes too

**RE-, UN-, IN-, DE-, PRE-** work the same way from the other end. RE- with a verb you have already found often gives a second word for free.

## 3. Hunt the pangram early

Every puzzle has at least one pangram, and it is worth seven extra points. Look for a long word that uses the unusual letters: the one J, X or Q often sits in the pangram. Writing the letters in a different order, or saying them out loud, helps you see it.

## 4. Repeat letters

Letters can be used more than once. Double letters (LL, EE, PP) are easy to forget and open up words like **APPEAL** or **LILAC**.

## 5. Use the hints, not the answers

When you are stuck, a hint grid tells you how many words start with each letter and how long they are, and the two-letter list shows how many begin with, say, CA. That narrows your search without spoiling it.

## 6. Know the word list

The daily puzzle uses a curated list. It leaves out proper nouns, hyphenated words and many obscure or offensive words, so a real word may still be rejected. If a word you know is not accepted, move on.
