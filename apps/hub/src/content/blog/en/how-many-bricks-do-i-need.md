Counting bricks is a matter of how many fit in a square foot of wall, joints included. Once you have that number, the rest is multiplication. Mortar is where the estimates differ more, and it helps to know why.

## Units per square foot

Each brick or block takes up its face plus one mortar joint along its length and one along its height:

**units per sq ft = 144 ÷ ((length + joint) × (height + joint))**, with sizes in inches.

| Unit | Face size | Joint | Per sq ft |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Modular brick | 7-5/8 × 2-1/4 in | 3/8 in | about 6.9 |
| Concrete block (CMU) | 15-5/8 × 7-5/8 in | 3/8 in | 1.125 |

You may also see 6.75 bricks per sq ft for modular brick. That figure assumes three courses rise exactly 8 in, which needs a slightly thicker bed joint.

## Worked example: a garden wall

A wall 20 ft long and 8 ft high with one 21 sq ft door opening:

1. Wall area: 20 × 8 − 21 = **139 sq ft**.
2. Modular brick: 139 × 6.86 = 954 bricks.
3. With 5% for breakage and cuts: **about 1,000 bricks**.

With 8 × 16 in concrete blocks the same wall needs 139 × 1.125 = 157 blocks, or **165** with 5% extra.

## Mortar

The mortar fills the bed joints (the horizontal ones) and the head joints (between units in a course) through the depth of the wall. For modular brick with 3/8 in joints that is about 0.055 cu ft per sq ft of wall, roughly 8 cu ft per 1,000 bricks. That is close to the figure the Brick Industry Association gives.

Bag charts are more generous: an 80 lb bag of premixed mortar makes about 0.6 to 0.7 cu ft but is usually quoted as laying 35 to 40 bricks. The difference is mortar lost while working, droppings and fuller joints. Treat the joint volume as the minimum and buy extra bags, especially if you are new to bricklaying.

## Blocks without vertical joints

Some blocks have interlocking ends, such as clay blocks like Porotherm and many aerated concrete blocks. They take mortar only in the bed joints, and aerated concrete is laid in thin-bed adhesive about 2 mm thick, which uses far less material. Use the adhesive's own coverage figure.

## Tips

- **Order from one batch.** Bricks from different batches can vary in colour.
- **Count corners and openings.** Half units, lintels and returns are not in the per-area figure.
- **Keep spares** for future repairs. Matching an old brick later is hard.
