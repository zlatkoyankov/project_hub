Gravel is sold two ways: by volume (cubic yards or cubic metres) and by weight (tons or tonnes). Suppliers switch between the two freely, and the conversion depends on the stone. That is where most ordering mistakes come from. This guide covers both, with examples you can repeat in the [gravel calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator/).

## Step 1: area times depth

Like any loose material, gravel volume is:

**volume = length × width × depth**

Convert the depth to the same unit as the length and width first:

- 3 inches = 0.25 ft, 4 inches = 0.333 ft, 6 inches = 0.5 ft
- 5 cm = 0.05 m, 10 cm = 0.10 m, 15 cm = 0.15 m

Then convert cubic feet to cubic yards by dividing by 27.

## Step 2: choose the depth

Depth depends on what the gravel has to carry. These are common starting points, not specifications:

| Use | Typical finished depth |
| --- | --- |
| Decorative cover over an existing base | 2 in (5 cm) |
| Garden path | 2–3 in (5–8 cm) |
| Driveway top layer over a compacted base | 3–4 in (8–10 cm) |
| New driveway, base plus top layer | often 6 in (15 cm) or more in total, in layers |

Driveways are usually built in layers: a coarser base stone that is compacted, then a finer top layer. Calculate each layer separately, because they are often different products with different densities.

## Step 3: convert volume to weight

The weight of a cubic yard of gravel depends on the stone, its size and how wet it is. A common rule of thumb is around 1.4 tons per cubic yard (about 2,700–2,800 lb), or roughly 1.6 tonnes per cubic metre. Crushed stone, pea gravel and road base all differ, so ask the supplier for the density of the exact product. The calculator lets you enter it.

## Example 1: a 50 × 10 ft driveway top layer, 4 inches deep

1. Depth: 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 ft
2. Volume: 50 × 10 × 0.333 = 166.7 cubic feet
3. Cubic yards: 166.7 ÷ 27 = **6.17 cubic yards**
4. Weight at 2,700 lb per cubic yard: 6.17 × 2,700 = 16,670 lb, or **about 8.3 tons**

Loose gravel settles and compacts once it is spread and driven on. Many people add 10–15% for compaction and uneven ground: 6.17 × 1.10 = 6.8 cubic yards, about 9.2 tons.

## Example 2: a garden path, 20 × 3 ft, 2 inches deep

1. Depth: 2 ÷ 12 = 0.167 ft
2. Volume: 20 × 3 × 0.167 = 10 cubic feet
3. Cubic yards: 10 ÷ 27 = **0.37 cubic yards**

That is a small amount. Bagged decorative gravel is often sold in bags of around 0.5 cubic feet, which would mean about 20 bags. A quarter or half yard of bulk gravel may be cheaper; compare prices with delivery included.

## Example 3: metric driveway, 15 × 3 m, 10 cm deep

1. Volume: 15 × 3 × 0.10 = **4.5 m³**
2. With 10% for compaction: 4.95 m³
3. Weight at 1.6 t/m³: 4.95 × 1.6 = **about 7.9 tonnes**

## Before you order

- **Prepare the ground.** Remove topsoil and roots where the driveway will go. Gravel laid on soft soil sinks and spreads, and you will be topping it up for years.
- **Consider edging.** Edges keep gravel in place, especially on paths next to lawns.
- **Think about a membrane.** A weed-control or stabilisation fabric under the gravel helps on some soils. It does not change the volume, but it changes how long the layer lasts.
- **Check delivery limits.** Trucks carry a maximum load and need space to tip. Ask before you order large amounts.
