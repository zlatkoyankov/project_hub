Percent means "per hundred". **15% of 200 is 200 × 15 ÷ 100 = 30.** Almost every percentage sum is one of the four below. The usual mistakes come from forgetting that a percent is always *of something*, and that something changes halfway through the sum.

## 1. What is X% of a number

**result = number × X ÷ 100**

Example: 15% of 200 = 200 × 15 ÷ 100 = **30**.

## 2. What percent is A of B

**percent = A ÷ B × 100**

Example: 30 out of 200 = 30 ÷ 200 × 100 = **15%**. Divide by the whole, not by the part.

## 3. Percent change

**change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100**

Example: a price went from $80 to $100.

- (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = **+25%**
- in money, the difference is $20

The other way round, from $100 to $80 is −20%, not −25%. You divide by the old value, and now the old value is different.

## 4. Price after a discount or markup

**new price = price × (1 ± X ÷ 100)**

Example: $50 with 20% off = 50 × 0.80 = **$40**. You save $10.

A markup works the same way with a plus. $50 with a 20% markup is 50 × 1.20 = $60.

## The mistakes everyone makes

**A rise and a fall by the same percent do not cancel out.** $100 + 20% = $120, then −20% = $96, not $100. The second 20% is taken from the bigger number.

**Two discounts do not add up.** "20% off and another 10% at the till" is not 30% off. The sum is 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72. You pay 72% of the price, so the discount is **28%**.

**Working backwards is not the same percent upwards.** If a price after 25% off is $60, the original was 60 ÷ 0.75 = **$80**, not 60 × 1.25 = $75. The same mistake happens when sales tax or VAT is taken off a total by subtracting the rate.

**A percent and a percentage point are different things.** If an interest rate goes from 3% to 4%, it has risen by **1 percentage point**, but by **33.3%** relative to where it was. Eurostat defines a percentage point as the simple difference between two percentages, while a percent change is relative to the starting value. When you read about rates, inflation or polls, check which one is meant.

## Quick table

| Sum | Formula | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X% of a number | number × X ÷ 100 | 15% of 200 = 30 |
| A as a % of B | A ÷ B × 100 | 30 of 200 = 15% |
| Change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 | 80 to 100 = +25% |
| Discount | price × (1 − X ÷ 100) | $50 − 20% = $40 |
