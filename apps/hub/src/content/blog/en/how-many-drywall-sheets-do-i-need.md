Drywall (plasterboard or gypsum board) comes in large sheets, and the number you need depends on surprisingly few measurements: the size of the room, the height of the walls and the sheet size you plan to hang. Here is how to work it out and what to watch for.

## Step 1: Wall area

The walls of a rectangular room together are as long as its perimeter.

**Wall area = 2 × (length + width) × wall height**

For a room 12 ft long and 10 ft wide with 8 ft walls:

2 × (12 + 10) × 8 = **352 square feet** of wall.

In metric, a 3.6 m × 3 m room with 2.5 m walls has 2 × 6.6 × 2.5 = 33 m².

## Step 2: Ceiling, if you are doing it

The ceiling is simply length × width: 12 × 10 = **120 square feet**. Ceilings are often hung with a different board (commonly thicker or sag-resistant), so many people order them separately.

## Step 3: Doors and windows

A standard interior door opening is about 3 ft by 7 ft, or 21 square feet (roughly 2 m²). Windows vary, so measure them.

Whether to subtract openings is a judgment call. Subtracting them gives the true area to cover, but offcuts from small openings are often too narrow to use. Many installers subtract only large openings, such as patio doors and big windows, and let the waste allowance absorb the rest.

For our room with one door: 352 − 21 = **331 square feet**.

## Step 4: Waste allowance

Around 10% is a common allowance for cuts, damaged edges and mistakes. Rooms with many corners, sloped ceilings or lots of openings need more.

331 × 1.10 = **364.1 square feet**.

## Step 5: Divide by the sheet area

| Sheet size | Area |
| --- | --- |
| 4 × 8 ft | 32 sq ft |
| 4 × 10 ft | 40 sq ft |
| 4 × 12 ft | 48 sq ft |
| 1.2 × 2.5 m | 3.0 m² |

With 4 × 8 ft sheets: 364.1 ÷ 32 = 11.4, which rounds **up to 12 sheets** for the walls.

Adding the 120 sq ft ceiling makes 451 sq ft to cover, or 496.1 sq ft with waste, which is **16 sheets** in total.

## Choosing the sheet size

Larger sheets mean fewer joints to tape and finish, which saves time and gives a flatter result. But a 4 × 12 ft sheet is heavy and awkward on your own and may not fit up a staircase. A good rule is to pick a length that matches your wall: 8 ft sheets on 8 ft walls hung vertically, or longer sheets hung horizontally to reduce joints.

## Studs, screws, tape and compound

The sheet count is only part of the shopping list. Common rules of thumb for the rest, using the same 12 × 10 ft room with 8 ft walls:

- **Studs** at 16 in on center along each wall, plus one at each wall's end: 10 on each 12 ft wall and 9 on each 10 ft wall, **38 studs** in all. With 8 ft walls, each stud is one 8 ft piece.
- **Floor and ceiling track**: twice the perimeter, 2 × 44 = 88 ft, or about 97 ft with waste, which is **13 pieces** of 8 ft.
- **Screws**: about one per square foot of board, so around **331**.
- **Joint tape**: about 370 ft per 1,000 sq ft (USG), roughly **122 ft**, so a single 250 ft roll is plenty.
- **Joint compound**: USG lists about 10 gallons of all-purpose compound per 1,000 sq ft, so one 4.5-gallon box covers about 450 sq ft: **1 box**.

These are estimates. Extra studs around doors and windows, ceiling framing and corner bead are not included, and your local building code may require particular boards in some places, such as fire-rated board next to a garage or moisture-resistant board in bathrooms. Check those requirements and your manufacturer's figures before you buy.
